01

Six real instruments

Correct faces and markings, not icons. Cessna 172 airspeed arcs, a three-pointer altimeter, a rotating compass card.

02

One shared flight state

The altimeter, VSI and turn coordinator cannot disagree, because every needle reads the same simulated aeroplane.

03

Set it, or fly it

Direct mode moves one parameter at a time. Fly mode hands you throttle, elevator, aileron and rudder.

04

Real instrument physics

Rate of turn is ω = 1091·tan(bank)/TAS, so a standard-rate turn really is 3°/s and the bank needed rises with speed.

05

Presets that teach

Straight and level, steady climb, descent, standard-rate turns, a climbing turn, and an approach to the stall.

06

Runs with no network

Install it once from the browser. It opens full screen and keeps working offline on a phone or a shared classroom PC.

No app store and no download. Once installed it opens full screen and still works with no network, so it runs on a shared classroom PC or on a student's own phone. Nothing is collected and there is no login.

The gauges above are drawn by the app itself, shown in a standard-rate climbing turn. The flight model is a simplified response model built to teach instrument behaviour, not a certified flight-dynamics model. Airspeed markings follow Cessna 172S values and IAS is treated as TAS; every instrumentation assumption is listed inside the app.
