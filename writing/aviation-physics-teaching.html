Teaching Aviation Physics: Bridging Theory and Practice in the Cockpit

Shahida Maqsood · December 2024 · Aviation Education, Physics, CAR Part 147

After years of teaching physics to aviation students within the CAR Part 147 framework, I have found that success lies not in understanding formulas, but in helping students visualise how physics governs every aspect of flight.

The challenge: making physics relevant

One of the greatest challenges in aviation physics education is bridging the gap between abstract mathematical concepts and their real-world applications in aircraft systems. Students often struggle to see how Newton's laws translate into lift generation, or how thermodynamic principles govern jet engine performance.

In my classroom at Abu Dhabi Polytechnic, I have developed approaches that turn these abstract concepts into tangible principles students can relate directly to their future careers in aviation maintenance and engineering.

Practical teaching strategies

Hands-on laboratory sessions

Our laboratory work includes experiments with wind tunnels, pressure measurement devices and electrical circuit analysis that directly mirror aircraft systems. Students measure, calculate, and verify theoretical predictions against real data.

Key physics concepts in aviation

The CAR Part 147 curriculum covers the physics domains every aviation maintenance engineer must master:

Fluid dynamics

Airflow, lift generation, drag forces

Thermodynamics

Engine cycles, heat transfer, efficiency

Mechanics

Forces, moments, structural loads

Electromagnetism

Avionics, navigation, communication systems

Student outcomes

The most rewarding part of this approach is the moment a student suddenly sees how the physics applies to an aircraft system. I have watched students who initially struggled with basic mechanics become confident analysing complex aircraft structural problems.

Many former students now work as licensed aircraft maintenance engineers across the UAE, and have told me that a strong physics foundation helped them in their professional certifications and in daily troubleshooting.

Looking forward

As aviation technology evolves with electric aircraft, advanced materials and digital systems, physics education has to evolve with it. I am developing curriculum modules that address emerging technologies while keeping the fundamental physics foundation intact.

The arrival of AI and digital twins in aviation maintenance opens further possibilities for teaching, letting students experiment with virtual aircraft systems and see immediate results from their calculations.

Teaching aviation physics is not about formulas and calculations — it is about preparing the next generation of aviation professionals to keep aircraft safe in the sky through a deep understanding of the principles that make flight possible. Shahida Maqsood
About the author. Shahida Maqsood is a Physics and Mathematics Instructor at Abu Dhabi Polytechnic with over 20 years of teaching experience and specialised expertise in CAR Part 147 aviation education. She is currently pursuing a PhD in Physics at UAEU, focusing on nanomaterials research.
